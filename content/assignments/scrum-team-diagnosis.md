You've just joined ShopEasy as Scrum Master for the **Basket team**: a Product Owner, Lena, and five Developers. They run two-week Sprints. Here's what you notice in your first two Sprints:

- **Work keeps rolling over.** In each of the last four Sprints the team finished about half of what it planned. Several items were "90% done" at the end and moved into the next Sprint.
- **Urgent requests arrive mid-Sprint.** The marketing manager, Raj, regularly messages Developers directly with "quick" urgent changes. Last week two Developers spent three days on a promotion banner. The Sprint Goal – *"Customers can save their basket and come back to it later"* – wasn't met.
- **Lena is hard to reach.** She's also running a supplier project, so the Developers often guess what she wants.
- **The Daily Scrum has become a status report.** Each Developer tells the Scrum Master what they did yesterday – a habit from the previous Scrum Master that continues with you. It often runs to 30 minutes.
- **The test environment is unreliable.** It crashes two or three times a Sprint and takes half a day to restore. The team has mentioned it at three Retrospectives in a row.
- **Testing piles up at the end.** The team's tester, Asha, usually receives most of the items in the last two days.

## What to produce

**Part A – Diagnosis.** In a few paragraphs, describe the main problems and their likely causes. Link what you see to the Scrum Guide (2020) – for example accountabilities, the Sprint Goal, the Definition of Done or empiricism. Avoid blaming individuals.

**Part B – Impediment log.** Log at least **three** impediments. For each, give its impact on the team, an owner (who will act on it – not always you), a specific next step and a status.

**Part C – Retrospective plan.** Plan a **60-minute** Sprint Retrospective for the Basket team. Give it a goal, then list the parts of the session with the stage, the activity, the minutes and why you chose it.

*Common workplace practice (optional):* the form uses a popular five-stage outline for retrospectives – set the stage, gather data, generate insights, decide what to do, close. It isn't part of the Scrum Guide, but it's a helpful structure.

## Tips
- Remember what a Scrum Master is accountable for – and what belongs to the Product Owner and the Developers.
- A good impediment has an owner who can actually act on it, and a next step someone could do this week.
- In the retrospective, give everyone a way to contribute, and make sure the team – not you – chooses the improvements.
