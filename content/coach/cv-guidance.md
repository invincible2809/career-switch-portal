### Be honest about what the course is

Employers value honesty. Describe this course as **self-directed learning with simulated projects**, not as a qualification or a job.

- Put it under **Training and development** or **Projects**, not under **Employment**.
- Say the projects used a **fictional company**. Interviewers will ask about them, and that's a good thing – it's a chance to explain your thinking.
- Don't use words like "certified" or "accredited". The course is aligned with the Scrum Guide (2020) and ISTQB Foundation terminology, but it doesn't award any certification or qualification.

### Aim for a realistic first step

Many Scrum Master roles expect experience of working in or with delivery teams. A stepping-stone role can build that experience – for example project coordinator, delivery or PMO assistant, junior business analyst, service desk analyst, UAT tester or junior manual tester.

### Quick checklist

- One page if you can, two at most.
- A short profile at the top: the role you're aiming for and two or three strengths.
- Numbers where they're true; never invent them.
- Every claim is something you'd happily explain in an interview.
