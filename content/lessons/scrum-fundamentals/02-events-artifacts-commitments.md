## Learning objective {#objective}
By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- describe the purpose of each of the five Scrum events and their time limits
- describe the three artifacts and the commitment that goes with each
- explain what can and can't change during a Sprint

This lesson takes about 20 minutes.

## Explanation {#explanation}
### The five events
Each event is a chance to inspect something and adapt. Every event has a **[[timebox]]** – a maximum length. The times below are the maximums for a one-month Sprint. For shorter Sprints, Planning, Review and Retrospective are usually shorter. The Daily Scrum is always 15 minutes.

| Event | Purpose | Timebox |
|---|---|---|
| **The Sprint** | The container for all the other events. Turns ideas into value. | One month or less |
| **[[Sprint Planning]]** | The Scrum Team agrees *why* this Sprint is valuable (the Sprint Goal), *what* can be done and *how* the work will get done. | Up to 8 hours |
| **[[Daily Scrum]]** | The Developers inspect progress towards the Sprint Goal and adapt their plan for the next day. | 15 minutes |
| **[[Sprint Review]]** | The Scrum Team and stakeholders inspect what was achieved, discuss what has changed and decide what to do next. A working session, not just a demo. | Up to 4 hours |
| **[[Sprint Retrospective]]** | The Scrum Team looks at how it worked – people, interactions, processes, tools, the Definition of Done – and picks improvements. | Up to 3 hours |

The Daily Scrum is for the Developers. It isn't a status report to a manager or to the Scrum Master.

### The three artifacts and their commitments
Each artifact has a **commitment** that gives it focus.

| Artifact | What it is | Commitment |
|---|---|---|
| **[[Product Backlog]]** | An ordered list of what's needed to improve the product | **[[Product Goal]]** – the long-term objective |
| **[[Sprint Backlog]]** | The Sprint Goal, the items chosen for the Sprint and the plan for delivering them | **[[Sprint Goal]]** – the single objective for the Sprint |
| **[[Increment]]** | A usable step towards the Product Goal | **[[Definition of Done]]** – the quality standard every Increment must meet |

Work that doesn't meet the Definition of Done isn't part of an Increment. It goes back to the Product Backlog to be considered later.

### During a Sprint
The Scrum Guide says that during the Sprint:

- no changes are made that would endanger the Sprint Goal
- quality does not decrease
- the Product Backlog is refined as needed
- scope may be clarified and renegotiated with the Product Owner as more is learned

Only the Product Owner can cancel a Sprint, and only if the Sprint Goal has become obsolete.

**[[Refinement]]** – breaking down and clarifying backlog items – is an ongoing activity, not one of the five events.

*Common workplace practice (optional):* many teams also use story points, velocity charts or burndown charts to help planning. These aren't part of the Scrum Guide.

## At ShopEasy {#workplace-example}
ShopEasy's checkout team runs two-week Sprints.

- **Sprint Planning (Monday morning, about 3 hours):** Priya, the Product Owner, explains that customers are abandoning their baskets on phones. The team agrees the Sprint Goal: *"Customers can check out on a phone without zooming or scrolling sideways."* The Developers pick the backlog items that serve that goal and plan the first few days of work.
- **Daily Scrum (every day, 15 minutes):** the Developers look at the Sprint Goal and ask what's in the way. On day 4 they realise the address form is bigger than expected, so two of them pair on it.
- **Sprint Review (last Thursday):** the Scrum Team, including Priya, shows the new phone checkout to two people from customer support and the marketing manager, and they discuss what to do next. Support mentions that older customers struggle with the small "Pay" button. Priya adds an item for it to the Product Backlog.
- **Sprint Retrospective (last Friday):** the team notices that testing piled up at the end of the Sprint. They agree to try finishing and testing one item before starting the next.

The team's Definition of Done includes: *code reviewed, automated tests passing, tested on two phone sizes, accessible by keyboard.*

## Worked example {#worked-example}
Halfway through a Sprint, these things happen at ShopEasy. Let's work out what Scrum says about each.

1. **A developer finds the address form needs more work than expected.** That's normal. The Developers adapt their plan at the Daily Scrum, and can clarify or renegotiate scope with Priya – as long as the Sprint Goal is still met.
2. **Marketing asks for an unrelated banner to go live on Friday.** Adding it would pull people away from the Sprint Goal. The request goes to Priya; she can add it to the Product Backlog for a future Sprint.
3. **Someone suggests skipping the accessibility checks to finish faster.** No – quality doesn't decrease during a Sprint, and accessibility is part of the Definition of Done.
4. **The company decides to replace the whole checkout with a new supplier's system next week.** The Sprint Goal may now be obsolete. Priya, as Product Owner, could cancel the Sprint.

## Guided practice {#guided-practice}
At ShopEasy's Daily Scrum, the Developers take turns telling Tom, the Scrum Master, what they did yesterday. Tom writes it down for the manager.

What's going wrong here?

:::hint
Who is the Daily Scrum for, and what is it for?
:::

:::hint
The Daily Scrum is for the Developers to inspect progress towards the Sprint Goal and adapt their plan. It has become a status report to Tom. Tom could coach the team to focus on the Sprint Goal and talk to each other, and step back from leading it.
:::

Which commitment would help the team decide whether an item is really finished?

:::hint
It's the commitment that goes with the Increment.
:::
